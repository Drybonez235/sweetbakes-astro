Planning an event can be stressful, especially when you need to accommodate food allergies. Here are some tips to help you work with a venue and get through these two common problems:

1. **The venue cannot accommodate food allergies.**
2. **The venue doesn’t allow outside food.**

---

## Step 1: Ask the Venue What They Can Safely Provide

Be clear about the food allergies involved and the procedures you need the venue to follow to safely accommodate them.

### Questions to Ask:
* Can your kitchen accommodate the specific allergy?
* What procedures do you have in place to prevent cross-contamination?
* Can you prepare the food using separate equipment?
* Do you have a separate preparation area?
* Can you provide ingredient information for the food being served?
* Can you accommodate the allergy for every meal course?
* Can you safely prepare the dessert?
* What happens if you cannot safely provide an allergy-friendly option?

If the venue can safely provide an appropriate option, that’s wonderful! **Get it in writing.**

> I have heard of situations where a venue employee told a client they could accommodate a food allergy, only for the client to be told months later—after contracts had been signed—that the venue could not accommodate them.

Be clear about what you need, and get the venue’s allergen policy and accommodations in writing.

---

## Step 2: Ask About the Outside Food Process

Most venues, in my experience, will allow outside food to be brought in when they are unable to accommodate specific food allergies. However, they may have some requirements.

### Common Venue Requirements:
* The food to come from a licensed vendor
* Ingredient or allergen information
* Proper labeling
* Advance notice
* Specific storage requirements
* A designated person to receive the food
* Separate serving utensils
* A plan for preventing cross-contact and contamination

These requirements aren’t a problem. Rather, they can be a good sign that the venue is taking food allergies seriously and has procedures in place to help keep everyone safe.

## What If the Venue Says, “No Outside Food”?

Ask if the venue has an accommodation process. A general “No outside food” policy isn’t necessarily the end of the conversation.

### You Can Ask:
> “Does the venue have a process for requesting reasonable accommodations under the ADA?”

The **Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)** requires covered businesses to make reasonable modifications to their policies, practices, and procedures when necessary to provide people with disabilities equal access to their goods and services, unless the requested modification would fundamentally alter the nature of what the business provides.

*Food allergies can be considered a disability if the allergy substantially limits one or more major life activities or major bodily functions.*

### Does This Mean a Venue Has to Let You Bring Any Food You Want?

**No.** The ADA requires *reasonable modifications*, not unlimited accommodations.

A venue may reasonably want to know:
* What food is being brought in?
* Where is it coming from?
* Who prepared it?
* How will it be stored?
* Will it require refrigeration?
* Who will handle it?
* Does the venue have food-safety requirements that still need to be followed?

These are different questions from simply saying, “We don’t allow outside food.”

You can also ask to speak with the venue manager or event director. Keep the conversation professional and document what was discussed. If the venue agrees to make an exception and allow outside food, they may require information similar to what was listed above for outside vendors.

---

## What Should You Do If Your Venue Still Says No?

If a venue denies an accommodation request, ask them to explain why. 

The ADA does not require every requested modification. A covered business may decline a modification if it can demonstrate that the modification would fundamentally alter the nature of its goods or services. Legitimate safety requirements may also be considered when they are based on actual risks.

*The circumstances matter.*

If you believe you are being denied access because of a food allergy, you may want to seek advice from an attorney or an appropriate disability-rights organization about your specific situation.

In my opinion, if a venue is being difficult or is unable to accommodate your needs, it’s best to find a different venue. The peace of mind you’ll get from being at a venue that is open about its policies and willing to work with you is worth more than any view, setting, or other feature that a more difficult venue may offer.
